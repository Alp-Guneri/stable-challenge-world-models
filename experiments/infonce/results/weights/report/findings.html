InfoNCE findings: how strongly to weight InfoNCE

experiments/infonce/results/weights · 27 Nov 2026 · reference architecture (cnn-mlp) · 3 seeds × 32,768 steps per combination, float32, batch 128 · held-out metrics on 1,332 windows from the 333 reserved episodes (proxy_eval.py, action_diagnostic.py)

Complete: all 19 combinations, 3 seeds each. ref (InfoNCE 1), ref_nce05x and ref_nce2x at MSE 25 are reused from the ablations' loss suite. All numbers are the team's local proxies on held-out training-corpus episodes, not the hidden leaderboard evaluation.
In short
  • A stronger InfoNCE term raises retrieval accuracy. At the reference MSE weight of 25, held-out top-1 goes from 73.0% (ref, InfoNCE 1) to 77.4% at InfoNCE 8 and 77.7% at InfoNCE 16, the best result in these experiments so far. The previous best was 74.9% (lewm_nce, loss suite).
  • The gain levels off from InfoNCE 8. InfoNCE 8, 16 and 32 are within 0.4 points of each other, about the seed-to-seed range.
  • It costs some motion accuracy. Motion R² falls from 0.944 to 0.913 at InfoNCE 16, and normalized MSE rises from 0.067 to 0.086. The local blend still improves slightly (1.025 → 1.041).
  • Keep the MSE weight at 25. Lowering it to 12 or 6 lowers top-1 at every InfoNCE weight from 4 up, and worsens motion R² and normalized MSE.
  • The gain is largest at long horizons. At InfoNCE 16, top-1 rises 2.2 points 5 steps ahead and 5.9 points 25 steps ahead.

1. What was compared

The ablations' loss suite found that InfoNCE drives most of the reference loss's advantage: removing it drops held-out top-1 from 73.0% to 38.8%. Halving or doubling its weight moved top-1 by about 1 point, so this experiment searches much wider. It trains the reference architecture (CNN encoder, MLP predictor) with every combination of

  • InfoNCE weight 0.5, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 (the reference uses 1), and
  • MSE weight 25, 12, 6 (the reference uses 25),

with the rest of the reference loss unchanged: InfoNCE temperature 0.1, VCReg 1, VCReg within clip 1. Presets are named ref_nce<w>x at MSE 25 and ref_nce<w>x_mse<m> otherwise, with 0.5 written 05.

Both networks are optimized with Adam and no gradient clipping, so multiplying every weight by the same factor would change little. What the grid varies is the balance between MSE and InfoNCE, and how strong both are relative to VCReg, whose weight stays 1.

5. Lowering the MSE weight does not help

At every InfoNCE weight from 4 up, MSE 25 gives the highest top-1, MSE 12 the next and MSE 6 the lowest. For example, at InfoNCE 16: 77.7%, 76.9% and 75.8%. Motion R² and normalized MSE are also worse at lower MSE weights. At InfoNCE 0.5 the MSE weight makes no difference (72.3%, 72.2%, 72.3%).

So the improvement comes from strengthening InfoNCE, not from weakening the MSE term. MSE weights above 25 were not tested; given this trend, they are a natural next step.

6. One collapsed seed

Seed 0 of ref_nce2x_mse12 (MSE 12, InfoNCE 2) scored 1.1% top-1; its other two seeds scored 74.3% and 74.6%. It collapsed within the first 500 training steps: the embeddings' effective rank fell to about 1, and InfoNCE stayed near 4.85 (ln 128, chance for a batch of 128) for all 32,768 steps. Its evaluation ran normally. No other seed in the grid collapsed, and the neighbouring cells are stable, so this looks like an unlucky start rather than a property of that setting. It does show that the early collapse phase seen in the ablations can still trap a run.

7. Caveats

  • Local proxies only. The hidden evaluation may weight motion accuracy differently from the local blend, and that is what a higher InfoNCE weight trades away.
  • Three seeds per setting. Differences under about 0.5 points of top-1, such as InfoNCE 8 vs 16 vs 32, are within seed noise.
  • One architecture. Everything here is cnn-mlp; the best weights may differ for other encoders or predictors.
  • Temperature was held at 0.1. Weight and temperature interact, and the loss suite only tested 0.05 and 0.2 at weight 1.

8. What this suggests

  • Use InfoNCE 8–16 with MSE 25. That gives about 77.5% top-1 against 73.0% for the reference. InfoNCE 8 has nearly all the gain with a smaller increase in normalized MSE (0.075 vs 0.086); InfoNCE 16 has the highest top-1 and blend by a margin within noise.
  • Test higher MSE weights (e.g. 50) at InfoNCE 8–16. Lowering MSE hurt, so raising it may win back some of the motion accuracy.
  • Combine with the other good loss changes: lewm_nce (74.9%) and ref_novcregt (74.6%) were the best in the loss suite. Neither was tried with a stronger InfoNCE term.
