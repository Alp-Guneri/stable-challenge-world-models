Ablation findings: encoder × predictor × loss

results/main · 22 Nov 2026 · 3 seeds × 32,768 steps per combination, float32, batch 128 · held-out metrics on 1,332 windows from the 333 reserved episodes (proxy_eval.py, action_diagnostic.py)

Snapshot, 8 of 9 combinations. cnn-ar-lewm crashed at its first step (a temporary-file error on NFS, since fixed) and is rerunning; this page does not include it. All numbers are the team's local proxies on held-out training-corpus episodes, not the hidden leaderboard evaluation.
In short
  • The encoder decides almost everything. Every ViT combination reaches 22–25% held-out top-1, whatever the predictor or loss; the CNN combinations reach 50–73%.
  • The reference loss helps, but only with the CNN encoder (73% vs 58%); with the ViT the two losses are within 1–3 points.
  • LeWM's transformer predictor does not help and is unstable with the CNN: two of three seeds of cnn-ar-ref got stuck at 39%, one reached 72%.
  • Predictor history helps motion, not retrieval: the newest-frame-only MLP matches the history MLP on top-1 but loses on motion R² (0.85 vs 0.94).
  • The reference model is still the best (cnn-mlp-ref, 73.0%, identical across seeds). Next gains are more likely from the encoder than from the predictor.

The gap is there from the first step

One step (5 environment steps) ahead, the ViT models retrieve the right future only 30–33% of the time, versus 89% for cnn-mlp-ref. The gap is not errors compounding over the rollout: even a single prediction is much harder to tell apart from other futures.

The ViT is converged, not undertrained

Its in-batch retrieval, losses and embedding rank flatten by about 15k steps, so more steps alone would not close the gap. Its embeddings also use fewer directions: effective rank ends around 25–40 (of 96 dimensions) versus about 100 (of 256) for the CNN.

3. The loss matters only with the CNN encoder

  • CNN + MLP: reference loss 73.0% ± 0.0 vs LeWM loss 57.7% ± 3.7 top-1, with lower normalized MSE (0.067 vs 0.087) and better use of actions (recorded actions ranked first of 8 at 25 steps: 92.7% vs 85.2%).
  • ViT: within 1–3 points of top-1 for both predictors. InfoNCE + VCReg pay off only when the encoder can use them.
  • Normalized MSE is lower with the reference loss for every architecture (0.05–0.08 vs 0.08–0.12).

4. LeWM's transformer predictor is unstable with the CNN encoder

cnn-ar-ref's mean of 50% hides two outcomes: seeds 0 and 1 got stuck at a lower plateau (in-batch top-1 ≈ 0.49, held-out 39%), while seed 2 reached the same curve as the reference model (72%). With the ViT encoder the transformer is slightly better than the MLP (24.9% vs 22.1% under the reference loss), so it is not useless, but it gives no reliable gain and needs its seed sensitivity understood before further use.

5. History in the predictor helps motion, not retrieval

vit-mlp1-ref sees only the newest frame embedding, so it cannot see velocity. It matches vit-mlp-ref (which sees 3 frame embeddings) on top-1 (24.9% ± 0.1 vs 22.1% ± 0.2) but is clearly worse on motion R² (0.853 ± 0.004 vs 0.935 ± 0.002). See the Motion R² panel in the held-out figure above.

6. The other metrics

  • Top-1 measures dynamics, not episode recognition. Retrieval from z[:,0] with no prediction (encoder_only) is about 1% for every model.
  • Motion R² is near its ceiling (≈0.94) for most models; it only separates the newest-frame MLP (0.85), one vit-ar-ref seed (0.83) and the two stuck cnn-ar-ref seeds (0.63–0.65). That is why the blend mostly tracks top-1.
  • Every model uses the actions. Recorded actions beat 7 action sequences from other episodes 93% (cnn-mlp-ref), 85% (cnn-mlp-lewm) and 73–80% (ViT) of the time; chance is 12.5%.
  • Ignore pixel R². It is strongly negative (−50 to −750) for every run: that probe does not transfer to held-out episodes. The README's blend leaves it out too.

7. Training dynamics: an early collapse phase

Several combinations spend their first thousands of steps collapsed (all embeddings near one point, effective rank ≈ 1) before recovering: cnn-mlp-lewm and cnn-ar-ref until about step 2,500–4,300, and vit-ar-lewm in some seeds until about step 1,200, after which it stalls near rank 10 until about step 5,500. cnn-mlp-ref leaves collapse within about 300 steps. The timing does not explain cnn-ar-ref's outcome: its best seed was the last to recover.

8. Caveats

  • Learning rates, schedules and loss weights were kept from each network's original model, not tuned for the mixed combinations; weak cnn-ar and ViT results may partly reflect that.
  • All runs are float32 (the originals used bf16); this made little difference for the two reproduced models.
  • Three seeds give a spread, not a confidence interval.
  • Local proxies only; the hidden evaluation may weigh things differently.
  • cnn-ar-lewm is missing from this snapshot.

9. What this suggests

  • Keep the CNN encoder and the reference loss: cnn-mlp-ref is the best and most stable combination.
  • Work on the encoder, not the predictor. Predictor and history choices move top-1 by a few points; the encoder moves it by 25–50. A ViT that takes the 3 frames stacked as input (like the CNN) would show whether the gap comes from seeing the frames together or from the ViT itself.
  • Before reusing the transformer predictor, find out why its seeds diverge (warm-up, learning rate, a residual connection).
