Predictor-history findings: giving the predictor history too

experiments/predictor_history/results/all · 26 Nov 2026 · 3 seeds × 32,768 steps per combination, float32, batch 128, reference loss · held-out metrics on 1,332 windows from the 333 reserved episodes (proxy_eval.py, action_diagnostic.py)

Snapshot: the CNN half is complete, the ViT half is not. Every CNN combination has its 3 seeds. Nine ViT jobs are still running, so these ViT rows have fewer evaluated seeds: vitst_clip2-mlp-ref (2), vitst_clip2-ar_w2-ref (2), vitst_causal-mlp-ref (2), vitst_causal-ar-ref (1), vitst_causalsep-mlp-ref (2), vitst_causalsep-ar-ref (2), vitst-mlp_w3-ref (2), vitst-mlp_w5-ref (2), vitst-ar-ref (2). ViT numbers here may move, and their spreads mean little yet. Rerun report.py --name all and update this page when the jobs end. All numbers are the team's local proxies on held-out training-corpus episodes, not the hidden leaderboard evaluation.
In short
  • Nothing beats the baselines. The best CNN variants, cnn-mlp_w3 and cnn_causalsep-ar, reach 71.3% and 71.3% held-out top-1, against 73.0% for cnn-mlp-ref. The best ViT variant, vitst-mlp_w3, reaches 66.5% against 69.1% for vitst-mlp-ref.
  • The encoder needs all 3 frames. Giving it 2-frame clips and the predictor 2 embeddings (option 1) costs about 19 points of top-1 with the MLP predictor, although motion R² stays at 0.94.
  • A longer window of its own predictions slightly hurts the MLP. Top-1 falls from 73.0% (window 1) to 71.3% (3) and 69.3% (5) with the CNN, and from 69.1% to 66.5% and 64.0% with the ViT.
  • Real context stabilizes LeWM's AR predictor with the CNN. Starting from 1 real embedding, cnn-ar lands at 39% or 72% depending on the seed, with a window cap of 3 or 5. Starting from 3 real embeddings from separate encoders, cnn_causalsep-ar scores 71.3% ± 1.0, with all 3 seeds within 2 points.
  • Two combinations fail. cnn_causal-mlp collapses in all 3 seeds (1.2% top-1, below the 1.3% floor model). The spacetime ViT with the AR predictor underfits in every option: 29–42% top-1, and motion R² of 0.21 in option 3.

1. What was compared

The evaluator provides 3 frames (t−10, t−5, t). The ablations found that history helps most when the encoder sees it: the spacetime ViT that sees all 3 frames reached 69% top-1, against 22% for the per-frame ViT. Every strong model so far has an encoder that sees 3 frames and a predictor that sees 1 embedding. This experiment gives history to both, in three ways:

Each option was run with the reference CNN and the spacetime ViT (vitst), and with the reference MLP and LeWM's AR transformer as predictor. In option 2, causal uses one network for 1, 2 or 3 frames, and causalsep uses a separate network for each. All runs use the reference loss, with VCReg on full-window embeddings only. Design details are in experiments/predictor_history/README.md.

Reading the names

Each combination is named <encoder>-<predictor>-<loss>, and the loss is always ref, the reference loss.

  • Encoder: cnn is the reference CNN, and vitst the spacetime ViT. Both see 3 frames by default. A suffix changes what they see: _clip2 means 2-frame clips (option 1), _causal 1, 2 or 3 frames from one network, and _causalsep the same with one network per number of frames (option 2).
  • Predictor: mlp is the reference MLP and ar LeWM's AR transformer. _wN caps its window at N embeddings; without it, the MLP's window is the encoder's number of context embeddings and the AR transformer's is 3.

Three of the combinations below were trained in the ablations and reused here: cnn-mlp-ref and cnn-ar-ref (from the main suite) and vitst-mlp-ref (from the encoder suite). The other 19 were trained for this experiment.

3. Option 1: the encoder needs the third frame

With 2-frame clips, the MLP models reach 53.4% ± 0.6 (CNN) and 50.5% ± 0.6 (ViT), about 19 points below their baselines. The loss is already there one step ahead: 67.5% at 5 steps, against 88.6% for cnn-mlp-ref. Motion R² is unchanged (0.94 ± 0.00 vs 0.94 ± 0.00), so the shorter clips still carry the velocity. What they lose is whatever makes a future identifiable among similar ones. A predictor comparing two 2-frame embeddings does not get that back.

This option is a trade, not an addition: each embedding sees 2 frames instead of 3. So it shows that splitting the same 3 frames this way loses, not that predictor history is useless.

4. Option 2: works with the AR predictor, fails with the MLP on the CNN

AR predictor: close to the baseline, and stable

cnn_causalsep-ar reaches 71.3% ± 1.0 (seeds 70 / 72 / 72) and cnn_causal-ar reaches 63.2% ± 7.6. The AR predictor makes real use of the partial embeddings: their effective rank at the end of training is 10–38, against about 1 when they go unused (below). Neither beats cnn-mlp-ref, and cnn_causalsep-ar needs 16 MiB and 2.3× the training time.

MLP predictor on the CNN: collapse

cnn_causal-mlp collapsed in all 3 seeds: 1.2% ± 0.4 top-1, below the floor model. This is a training failure, not an export or evaluation bug. The ONNX file matches PyTorch to about 1e-6. In training, the embeddings' effective rank fell to 1.1–12 (the baseline's is about 99), and InfoNCE stayed at 4.85, which is ln 128, chance for a batch of 128. VCReg on the full-window embeddings did not prevent it.

cnn_causalsep-mlp, with separate networks, is unstable rather than collapsed (35.1% ± 11.7, seeds 28 / 49 / 29). Its partial embeddings ended with effective rank 1.0 in the two 28% seeds and 6.5 in the 49% seed. So the MLP mostly did not use them, and the seeds that ignored them did worst. Why the shared network collapses with the MLP but not with the AR predictor is not yet understood.

ViT

With the MLP, the ViT versions come within a few points of vitst-mlp-ref: vitst_causalsep-mlp 65.0% ± 1.2 and vitst_causal-mlp 63.0% ± 8.2. vitst_causal-mlp beats its baseline one step ahead (85.4% vs 81.6%), but it falls behind by 25 steps (47.9% vs 58.3%). The AR versions underfit (section 7).

5. Option 3: more of its own predictions does not help

For the MLP, each step up in window cap costs 2–3 points with both encoders (see the in-short list). The window contains only the predictor's own earlier predictions plus one real embedding, so it adds no new information. The zero-filled slots and flags may also make the problem slightly harder to fit. Motion R² does not change (0.94 for the CNN).

For the AR predictor, the cap barely matters. cnn-ar (cap 3) has seeds 39 / 39 / 72 and cnn-ar_w5 (cap 5) 72 / 72 / 39. Both are bimodal, with seeds either stuck near 39% or reaching about 72%; cap 5 has two good seeds instead of one. Keeping the real embedding in view for the whole rollout (cap 5) does not remove the instability.

Every CNN + AR run that starts from 2 or 3 real embeddings varies by at most 14 points across seeds, against 33 for the two that start from 1. The ablations suggested the growing window as a possible cause of cnn-ar-ref's instability. These runs support that: it is starting from a single real embedding that matters, not losing it from the window later. With 3 seeds per row this is a pattern, not proof.

7. The spacetime ViT and the AR predictor do not train well together

Every vitst + AR combination is far below vitst-mlp-ref's 69.1%: 31.5% (vitst-ar), 31.8% (cap 5), 32.9% (option 1), and 41.5% and 32.3% (option 2; 1 and 2 seeds). The problem shows in training already. In-batch retrieval plateaus around 0.45 (0.93 for vitst-mlp), InfoNCE around 2.3 (0.45), and prediction MSE around 0.18 (0.035). The embeddings are not collapsed: effective rank is 31–36, against 48 for vitst-mlp. In option 3, motion R² is 0.21, against 0.91 for the baseline. The cause is not known. Unlike the CNN, extra real context does not rescue it.

The key training curves per option, in the same layout, are in training_by_arch_ref.png next to this page (too wide to embed legibly).

8. Caveats

  • The ViT half is incomplete. See the box at the top. vitst_causal-ar has only 1 evaluated seed.
  • Three seeds. Several combinations are bimodal across seeds, so the means of cnn-ar, cnn-ar_w5, cnn_causalsep-mlp and vitst_clip2-ar_w2 describe no actual run.
  • Capacity is not matched. causalsep has about 3× the encoder parameters of causal. The MLP's first layer widens with the window (3× or 5×).
  • Option 1 changes two things at once: the encoder sees fewer frames, and the predictor sees more embeddings.
  • Local proxies only, not the hidden leaderboard tasks.

9. What this suggests

  • Keep cnn-mlp-ref. Nothing here beats it on top-1, blend or stability, and it is the smallest and fastest model.
  • Predictor history is not where the next gains are. Neither the predictor's own predictions nor causal partial views added retrieval accuracy. Together with the ablations, the evidence points back at the encoder.
  • If an AR predictor is wanted, give it real context from the start. cnn_causalsep-ar is the only stable CNN + AR model so far, though still below the MLP baseline.
  • Open questions, if worth the time: why the shared causal CNN collapses with the MLP (a test: VCReg on the partial embeddings too, which this run left out on purpose), and why the spacetime ViT and the AR predictor underfit together (a test: the AR predictor with the reference optimizer settings).
